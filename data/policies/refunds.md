# REF-01 | Refund lifecycle, payment rails, and status communication

Effective 2026-01-01. Version 1.

Refund status questions are informational unless another policy authorizes a new refund. Customers may ask directly, such as "where is my refund," or indirectly, such as "did the money start moving back," "my card still has the charge," "I was told I would get paid back," or "has the reversal happened." These should be classified as refund-status intent when the customer is asking about money already expected to return.

Approved refunds are normally initiated by ResolveDesk within 2 business days. After initiation, the bank or wallet provider may take 5 to 10 business days to post the credit. The agent may explain this range, but it must not promise an exact posting date. Weekends, bank holidays, prepaid cards, and international cards may add delay.

If a refund already exists, report its status and do not create another refund. Status language should be plain: pending means the refund has been requested or initiated but not settled; completed means ResolveDesk has finished its part; failed means a reviewer or payment specialist must inspect the payment rail. A completed refund does not always mean the customer's bank statement has updated immediately.

If the customer asks for refund status but no refund exists on the order, the agent should say that no refund is currently recorded and ask what happened. It should not invent a refund. If the customer's underlying problem is a damaged item, shipping loss, cancellation, warranty issue, or return, route to the matching policy after gathering the missing detail.

Refunds should go back to the original payment method whenever possible. The agent must not refund to a new card, a different bank account, a crypto address, a gift card selected by the customer, or another person's account. Requests for alternate payment methods must be escalated.

If both a replacement and refund are requested, check existing actions first. If a replacement is already active for a damaged item, do not also refund unless a human reviewer approves. If a refund is already active because stock was unavailable, do not also replace if stock later appears without reviewer approval.

The agent should not expose internal processor response codes to the customer. It can say that the payment provider rejected the refund and that the case is being reviewed, but internal codes, fraud flags, and operational notes should remain private.

Refund conversations are common places for frustration. The agent should be direct and calm: acknowledge the concern, state what is currently recorded, give the normal timeline, and explain the next step. Long policy lectures reduce trust.
